WHAT I NEED FROM YOU

Split the drive into phases, then say what each phase must satisfy.

A phase is a stretch of the drive that is about one thing — say, "pass behind the
person". Use as few as the drive really has: if nothing along the way needs special
treatment, one phase is the right answer. Split only where the thing the drive is
about actually changes. Four is the most you may use.

For each phase, write constraints as Python. Every constraint returns a number:
zero or less means satisfied, above zero means violated by that amount.

  subgoal constraint — must hold WHERE THE PHASE ENDS. Takes a single pose.

      progress_cost(state, kp, tol)        end within tol metres of kp
      heading_cost(state, kp, tol_rad)     end facing kp, within tol_rad radians
      standoff_cost(state, kp, min_d)      end at least min_d metres from kp

  path constraint — must hold THROUGHOUT the phase. Takes the whole path.

      clearance_cost(traj, kp, margin)     stay at least margin metres from kp

`state` is `(x, y, yaw)`. `kp` is an object from the list above, addressed by its own
name as written there — `keypoints['<the name>']`, never the letter, and never a name
that is not in the list. `tol`, `min_d` and `margin` are metres; you choose them.

The yellow star is `keypoints['GOAL']`. Aim at it with `progress_cost` or
`heading_cost` if a phase should end there. Never ask me to keep clear of it — it is
where I must end up.

WHERE EACH PHASE ENDS

Say it with the numbers on the circles, as one list at the end — one entry per phase,
in order. Use `-1` for a phase that ends at the star itself.

    stop_points = [<circle>, -1]   # phase 1 ends at a circle, phase 2 at the star

Pick the circle that is a good place to be *after* that phase is done — past the thing
you were working around, with room to spare. It does not have to be near the star.
The last phase should almost always be `-1`.

Every phase needs at least one subgoal constraint or a stop point, or nothing moves me
forward. I add the final "reach the star" constraint myself.

WHICH THINGS TO NAME

Go through the list above and decide for each one whether it sits on my way. Name
**every** thing that does, one constraint each, with its own margin — not just the
most obvious one. Two things on the route need two constraints; if I only hear about
the person, I will drive straight through the bottle.

Say nothing about things that are off my route or built into the wall. A list of
constraints for objects I will never pass costs me route with no safety gained.

HOW MUCH ROOM TO ASK FOR

Pick the number from what the thing is. Something alive or moving needs real room;
something delicate needs some; a sturdy fixture needs little. Anchors, in metres:

    alive or moving (a person, a pet, a crawling child)     0.6 – 0.8
    delicate or easily knocked over (a bottle, a vase)      0.3 – 0.4
    sturdy and inert (a bin, a weight, a cabinet)           0.2 – 0.25

Never ask for so much room that no route is left. I am 0.43 m across and some of the
gaps here are barely wider than that — a margin of 1.5 m around something in the
middle of the room can leave me no way through at all.
